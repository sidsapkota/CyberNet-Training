# Scam Texts (`scam-texts-and-calls`)

- **Module:** 2 · Spot the Scam · Pro

- **The story:** Sam, Sam's phone and the Parcels text thread (the `text-message` scene). Sam is waiting
  for a hoodie. Each card changes one thing: the text arrives, Sam looks closer, pays the fee, sees the
  signs named, finds them alone, then a text "from Alex" and a text "from Your Bank".

## 1 · Hook
- **Situation:** Sam's hoodie is on its way. Then Parcels texts.
- **Picture:** Sam's lock screen, "7:41 pm", one notification: a speech-bubble icon, "Parcels" in bold,
  and "We tried to deliver your parcel. Pay a $1.95 fee…". Next to it, a small drawing of a hoodie in
  a box with a tick: "Ordered". The notification has a soft pulse ring.
- **Action:** Tap the text to open it.

## 2 · Play
- **Situation:** Sam opens the Parcels thread. Tap each part to look closer.
- **Picture:** the existing `text-message` scene, full width: the sender name "Parcels", Monday's
  message ("Your parcel is on its way. Track it in the Parcels app."), "Today, 7:41 pm", then the new
  message: the $1.95 fee, "within 2 hours, or it goes back:", and the link
  "parcels-redelivery.example/pay". Five parts pulse twice. Callouts, pinned inside the scene:
  - **"Parcels":** "Anyone can make a text show a name like Parcels. That's how this one landed in the
    real thread."
  - **Monday's message:** "The real Parcels text: no link, just 'track it in the Parcels app'."
  - **The fee:** "A small fee at a link. Parcel 'fees' are one of the most common scam texts."
  - **"Within 2 hours":** "A hurry, so you pay before you think."
  - **The link:** "parcels-redelivery.example isn't the Parcels app. It's a page anyone could have made."
- **Action:** Tap each glowing part (Continue unlocks after all five). Not graded.

## 3 · Predict
- **Situation:** Sam taps the link and pays the $1.95 fee. What happens next?
- **Picture:** a pay page on Sam's phone ("parcels-redelivery.example" at the top, "Pay $1.95", card
  number boxes filled with "•••• •••• •••• 4821", a "Pay" button pressed). Below, three tappable
  objects: a delivery van with the hoodie box ("Hoodie arrives tomorrow"); a bank card with arrows
  leading away from it ("They can spend from the card"); a coin with a curved arrow ("Parcels refunds
  the $1.95").
  → **Reveal:** the card number travels to a laptop with a "parcels-redelivery.example" sticker. Then
  Sam's card shows a list of new spending Sam never made: "$89.00", "$240.00". The van stays parked,
  empty.
- **Action:** Tap what happens.
- **Answers:** hoodie arrives (van) · they can spend from the card (card) · Parcels refunds it (coin)
- **Correct:** they can spend from the card
- **Right:** The page is the scammer's, so now they have Sam's card number. The $1.95 was just bait.
- **1st wrong:** Who owns the page the link opened? And what did Sam type in to pay?
- **2nd wrong:** The card: the page belongs to the scammer, not Parcels, so they now have Sam's card
  number.

## 4 · Name it
- **Says:** A **scam** text copies a name you trust, then rushes you to pay or tap a link. Never use the
  text's link: check the real way, in the company's own app.
- **Picture:** the same thread, now with three amber tags on the new text: "Asks for money" ($1.95
  fee), "Rushing you" (2 hours), "Fake link" (the link). The name "Parcels" gets a small grey note, not
  a tag: "Names can be faked". Beside the phone, a big Parcels app icon
  (a box with a tick, no real logo) with a cyan arrow: "Check here". The app's screen shows "Hoodie: on
  its way. Nothing to pay."

## 5 · Easy win
- **Situation:** This new Parcels text is a scam. Find its three warning signs.
- **Picture:** the `text-message` scene, no tags; every part has a hollow ring.
- **Action:** Tap the three warning signs in the new text, then Check.
- **Answers:** the six parts of the thread
- **Correct:** the fee, "within 2 hours", the link (not the sender name, Monday's message or the time)
- **Right:** It asks for money, rushes you and sends you to a fake link.
- **1st wrong:** Only the new text counts. Look for money, a hurry and a web address.
- **2nd wrong:** The fee, the 2-hour deadline and the link. The sender name could be faked, but you
  can't see that by looking.

## 6 · Twist
- **Situation:** A text from a new number says it's Alex, Sam's friend. Now what?
- **Picture:** a new thread, header "Unknown number · Not in your contacts". One grey bubble: "Hey it's
  Alex! New number, lost my phone. Can you send me $40 for the train home? Quick pls". Below, three
  tappable objects: a money icon "Send the $40"; a reply bubble "Is it really you?"; a contact card
  "Alex (saved)" with a phone icon "Call Alex's saved number".
  → **Reveal:** the contact card call connects: "Alex (saved) · 0:05", and a bubble from the real Alex:
  "Huh? I'm home. That wasn't me!"
- **Action:** Tap what Sam should do.
- **Answers:** send the $40 · reply "Is it really you?" · call Alex's saved number
- **Correct:** call Alex's saved number
- **Right:** There's no app for a friend, so check the real way: the number you already have.
- **1st wrong:** The text asks for money and rushes you. What's the real way to reach Alex?
- **2nd wrong:** Call Alex's saved number. A reply goes to the new number, which could be the scammer.

## 7 · Real life
- **Situation:** A text says your card from Your Bank is blocked. Where do you check?
- **Picture:** a phone home screen. At the top, the text as a notification: "Your Bank: Card blocked.
  Unlock now: yourbank-unlock.example or call the number below". Below, three big tappable app icons
  in a row: Messages (with a "1" badge) "Tap the text's link"; Phone, with a note "Call the number in
  the text"; a Your Bank app icon (a plain shield-and-coin, no real logo) "Open the Your Bank app".
- **Action:** Tap where you'd check.
- **Answers:** the text's link (Messages) · the number in the text (Phone) · the Your Bank app
- **Correct:** the Your Bank app
- **Right:** The app is the real way in; the link and the number came from whoever sent the text.
- **1st wrong:** Which of these did the text not give you?
- **2nd wrong:** The Your Bank app. The scammer chose the link and the number, so neither is safe.

## 8 · Wrap
- **Takeaway:** A surprise text with a fee, a rush or a link? Check the real way, in the company's own
  app.
- **Try this:** Find one company text on your phone. Does it want you to tap a link?
- **Next:** A call in a voice you know, asking for gift cards.
