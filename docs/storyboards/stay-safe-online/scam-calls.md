# Scam Calls (`scam-calls`, new: split from `scam-texts-and-calls`)

- **Module:** 2 · Spot the Scam · Pro (new lesson, order 3, between Scam Texts and Fake Websites)
- **The one idea:** If a caller wants a code, money or for you to tell no one, hang up and call back on a
  number you already have.
- **The story:** Sam, Sam's phone and one call from "Your Bank's fraud team" (a new `phone-call` scene:
  the call screen with the caller's words as speech bubbles). Each card changes one thing: the phone
  rings, Sam listens, reads out the code, sees the signs named, sorts what callers say, then hears
  cousin Jess's voice, then has to pick a number to call back.
- **Cards:** 8 · difficulty: easy (3 Predict), easy (5 Easy win), medium (6 Twist), stretch (7 Real life)
- **From today's lesson:** new lesson id `scam-calls` (title "Scam Calls", icon `phone`,
  already on the allow-list and unused in this module). Moved here from `scam-texts-and-calls`:
  `hang-up-or-ok` (Easy win, one item added) and `sounds-like-cousin` (Twist, cousin now named Jess).
  Moved cards start fresh, since progress is keyed on lesson id plus card id. New: `the-phone-rings`
  (Hook), `listen-to-the-call` (Play), `read-out-the-code` (Predict), `meet-hang-up` (Name it),
  `which-number` (Real life), `recap` (Wrap). Dropped: `in-your-pocket` (its list is now the Play and
  Name it) and "control of your computer" as a sign (one story, four signs; it can come back in a later
  lesson if wanted).
- **Builds on:** Two-Step Sign-In (a sign-in code arrives when someone types your password; never share
  one), Scam Emails ("Rushing you", "Asks for secrets", a name proves nothing) and Scam Texts (check the
  real way; a saved number).
- **Needs building:**
  - A **hook card** (a full-width picture, one line, tap to start); until then card 1 can be a `reveal`
    on the new scene's caller line.
  - A **`phone-call` scene** (self-labelled, like `text-message`): an incoming-call screen with a caller
    line, up to four speech bubbles (the caller's words) and an End call button, each bubble its own
    part. Used in explore mode (Play), drawn labelled for Name it, and usable in tap mode for a quiz.
    Generic only, no real numbers (the caller line says "Unknown number").

## 1 · Hook
- **Situation:** Sam's phone rings. A calm voice says, "Your Bank fraud team."
- **Picture:** Sam's phone, incoming call screen: a grey silhouette circle, "Unknown number", "mobile",
  and two big round buttons at the bottom, red "Decline" and green "Accept". The green button has a soft
  pulse ring.
- **Action:** Tap Accept.

## 2 · Play
- **Situation:** Listen to the caller. Tap each line to look closer.
- **Picture:** the new `phone-call` scene: at the top "Unknown number · 0:42", then four speech bubbles
  from the caller, one under the other:
  1. "Someone's spending on your card right now!"
  2. "Read me the code we just texted you."
  3. "Then buy $300 of gift cards to keep it safe."
  4. "Don't tell anyone. Not even your family."
  At the bottom: Mute, Speaker and a red End call button. The caller line and the four bubbles pulse
  twice. Callouts, pinned inside the scene:
  - **"Unknown number":** "Anyone can say they're Your Bank. Saying a name proves nothing."
  - **Bubble 1:** "A scare to make you hurry. That's rushing you."
  - **Bubble 2:** "A code arrives when someone types your password. Your Bank never asks for it."
  - **Bubble 3:** "Gift cards are a scammer's favourite: once you send the codes, the money is gone."
  - **Bubble 4:** "'Tell no one' stops anyone helping you check."
- **Action:** Tap each glowing line (Continue unlocks after all five). Not graded.

## 3 · Predict
Predict: not scored (any answer, including Not sure, goes straight to the reveal; no hint, no 2nd-wrong step; not counted in first-try accuracy).
- **Situation:** Sam reads the texted code out to the caller. What can they do now?
- **Picture:** Sam's phone with the text "Your Bank code: 482 913. Never share this code." and a speech
  bubble from Sam "4… 8… 2…". Below, three tappable objects: a laptop showing a sign-in screen ("Sign
  in to Sam's account"); a card with a red "Payment stopped" stamp ("Stop the strange payment"); a code
  in a bin ("Nothing: a code alone is useless").
  → **Reveal:** the caller's laptop screen changes to "Your Bank · Welcome back, Sam". On Sam's phone a
  banner slides in: "New sign-in on another device".
- **Action:** Tap what the caller can do.
- **Answers:** sign in to Sam's account (laptop) · stop the payment (stamped card) · nothing (bin) ·
  "Not sure"
- **What happens:** the caller signs in to Sam's account.
- **Reveal line:** Here's what happens… the code was the last step of signing in, so reading it out lets the caller in.

## 4 · Name it
- **Says:** If a caller wants a code, money or for you to tell no one, it's a **scam**: hang up. Then
  call back on a number you already have, like the one on the back of your card.
- **Picture:** the same call screen, now with amber tags: "Rushing you" (bubble 1), "Asks for secrets"
  (bubble 2), "Asks for money" (bubble 3), "Tell no one" (bubble 4). The red End call button glows with
  a cyan ring: "Hang up". Beside the phone, the back of a bank card ("Your Bank" and a printed phone
  number drawn as a grey bar, no real digits) with a cyan arrow: "Call back here".

## 5 · Easy win
- **Situation:** Callers say all sorts of things. Which ones mean hang up?
- **Picture:** five speech-bubble chips in a tray, above two bins: a red phone "Hang up" and a green
  tick "That's OK". The chips:
  - "Read me the code we texted you."
  - "Pay in gift cards or lose your money."
  - "Don't tell your parents about this call."
  - "You call the number on the back of your card."
  - "Your friend calls to ask about homework."
- **Action:** Drag (or tap) each one into a bin, then Check.
- **Answers:** the five chips
- **Correct:** Hang up: the code, gift cards, "don't tell your parents". That's OK: calling the number on
  your card, your friend about homework.
- **Right:** Codes, gift cards and "tell no one" mean hang up; a number you chose is fine.
- **1st wrong:** Check each one: does it want a code, money, or for you to tell no one?
- **2nd wrong:** Hang up on the code, the gift cards and "don't tell". Calling your card's number and a
  homework call are fine.

## 6 · Twist
- **Situation:** An unknown number rings. It sounds just like Sam's cousin Jess.
- **Picture:** the call screen again, "Unknown number", with one big bubble in Jess's voice: "Sam, it's
  Jess! I'm in trouble. Buy gift cards and send me the codes. Tell no one!" A small sound-wave line under
  it. Below, three tappable objects: a gift card "Buy them: it's Jess's voice"; a finger to lips "Keep it
  secret, like Jess asked"; the red End call button plus a contact card "Jess (saved)" with "Hang up, call
  Jess's saved number".
  → **Reveal:** the call to "Jess (saved)" connects; the real Jess: "What? I'm at netball. That wasn't
  me!"
- **Action:** Tap what Sam should do.
- **Answers:** buy them · keep it secret · hang up and call Jess's saved number
- **Correct:** hang up and call Jess's saved number
- **Right:** Voices can be copied from a short clip, but Jess's saved number reaches the real Jess.
- **1st wrong:** Forget the voice. Which warning signs did you hear?
- **2nd wrong:** Hang up and call Jess's saved number: gift cards and "tell no one" are warning signs,
  whoever's voice it is.

## 7 · Real life
- **Situation:** "Your Bank" just rang about your card. Tap the number to call back.
- **Picture:** three tappable objects side by side: a sticky note with "The number the caller read out"
  (a scribbled grey line, no digits); the phone's Recents list with "Unknown number · 2 min ago" and a
  "Call back" button; the back of your bank card with "Your Bank · Lost or stolen? Call us" and a
  printed number drawn as a grey bar.
- **Action:** Tap the number you'd call.
- **Answers:** the caller's number (sticky note) · Call back (Recents) · the back of your card
- **Correct:** the back of your card
- **Right:** The card's number was there before the call, so the caller can't have chosen it.
- **1st wrong:** Which of these numbers did you already have before the phone rang?
- **2nd wrong:** The back of your card. The caller chose the other two, so they can lead straight back
  to the scammer.

## 8 · Wrap
- **Takeaway:** A caller wants a code, money or for you to tell no one? Hang up and call back on a
  number you already have.
- **Try this:** Save your bank's real number (from the back of your card or its own app) in your contacts.
- **Next:** A website that looks exactly like your bank's, but isn't.
